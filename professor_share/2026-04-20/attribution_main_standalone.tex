\noindent\textbf{Reading guide.}
\textbf{Suff@25\%} ($\uparrow$): similarity retained when only the top 25\% attributed tokens are kept. Higher is better.
\textbf{Comp@25\%} ($\uparrow$): similarity drop when those tokens are removed. Higher is better.
\textbf{Cmpct@0.8} ($\downarrow$): fraction of tokens needed to recover 80\% of the full-pair cosine. Lower is better.
$\boldsymbol{\rho_{\text{LOO}}}$ ($\uparrow$): rank correlation between each attribution's claimed importance and its true leave-one-out impact on the cosine. Higher is better.

\vspace{0.6em}

\noindent\textbf{Bottom line on this subset.}
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0em
\item $\rho_{\text{LOO}}$ improves under ABTT on every (model, method) cell shown here, and on every cell in the wider 4-model $\times$ 9-method sweep (8/8). This is the one robust signal.
\item The other three metrics (sufficiency, comprehensiveness, compactness) go the \emph{other} way under ABTT for IG on both T5 models (3/4 metrics worse). RetrievalMarK on PhilTa improves 3/4. RetrievalMarK on LaTa improves 1/4.
\item So the original sanity-check threshold ``ABTT improves $\geq$3/4 metrics for both MaRC and IG on at least one T5 model'' is \emph{not} met. Only PhilTa $\times$ MaRC clears it.
\item Caveat: a fraction of the apparent ``worsening'' is that ABTT changes both the cosine being explained and the attribution scores themselves, so cross-variant deltas mix two effects. With only 20 pairs per cell the numbers are descriptive, not statistically powered.
\end{itemize}
