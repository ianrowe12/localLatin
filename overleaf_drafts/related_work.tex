\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Anisotropy, outlier dimensions, and post-hoc correction.}
Contextual embeddings crowd into a narrow cone, usually measured by the mean cosine of random pairs \citep{gao2019degeneration, ethayarajh2019contextual}. That statistic mainly reflects a shared mean offset \citep{rudman2022isoscore}. A separate distortion comes from a few outlier dimensions of very large magnitude \citep{kovaleva2021bertbusters, puccetti2022outlier}, which dominate cosine similarity \citep{timkey2021rogue}. In decoder language models they appear as massive activations that emerge early and persist through the middle layers \citep{sun2024massive}. T5 encoders carry such outliers too, and their magnitude grows with depth \citep{zhao2025outlier}. Post-hoc corrections remove the mean and top principal directions \citep{mu2018allbutthetop}, whiten the space \citep{su2021whitening, huang2021whiteningbert}, or down-weight frequent tokens \citep{arora2017sif}. \citet{timkey2021rogue} apply such corrections at every layer of four pre-trained language models and recover word-similarity quality. After task fine-tuning, the dominant directions carry task information, and removing them hurts \citep{rajaee2021finetuning}. For final-layer embeddings, top-dimension variance share predicts when cosine fails \citep{parupudi2026anisotropy}, and effective rank is the most informative geometric predictor of retrieval quality, though the prediction is moderate \citep{rottach2025topology}. Geometric metrics do not reliably predict the downstream performance of language models \citep{kulkarni2026geometry}, and the cosine geometry of sentence embeddings does not predict their task performance \citep{nastase2025geometry}. None of these studies compares the statistics with retrieval quality layer by layer in T5 encoders. We make that comparison and fit the correction on training data only.

\paragraph{Layerwise similarity and retrieval quality.}
Intermediate layers often outperform the final layer on embedding tasks \citep{skean2025layer}. Layerwise geometry studies place the anisotropy peak in the middle layers of decoder models \citep{razzhigaev2024shape, machina2024anisotropy} and report flatter profiles for encoders \citep{razzhigaev2024shape}. \citet{haemmerl2023anisotropy} find a large outlier dimension in a middle layer of a multilingual encoder and improve cross-lingual retrieval by zeroing it. Evidence for T5 stops short of its encoder layers. Mean-pooled final-layer T5 embeddings trail fine-tuned encoders on semantic similarity \citep{ni2022sentencet5}. Whitening gives T5 large similarity gains, reported for an average of its first and last layers \citep{huang2021whiteningbert}. Layerwise anisotropy for T5 has been reported with encoder and decoder states concatenated \citep{godey2024anisotropy}.

\paragraph{Latin encoders and text reuse.}
Latin has dedicated encoder-only models, Latin BERT \citep{bamman2020latinbert} and LaBERTa and PhilBERTa, and the encoder-decoder models LaTa and PhilTa trained on the same data \citep{riemenschneider2023lata}. Work on allusive text reuse in Latin finds that distributional semantics moderately improves retrieval over lexical matching \citep{manjavacas2019allusive}. Recent benchmarks target intertextuality in classical Latin literature \citep{schelb2026locisimiles} and semantic search in Latin and Ancient Greek \citep{toyin2026gretino}. \citet{reichbauer2026alignment} mine parallel Latin and Greek sentences and find that whitening helps specialized encoders and slightly hurts LaBSE. Our testbed differs in its labels, which come from an edition of medieval canon-law witnesses, and in its question: where the retrieval signal sits inside each encoder.
