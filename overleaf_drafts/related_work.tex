\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Anisotropy and geometric post-processing.}
Embedding spaces from pre-trained models occupy narrow cones instead of filling their dimensions. The effect has been traced to likelihood training over long-tailed vocabularies \citep{gao2019degeneration}, measured across layers in contextual models \citep{ethayarajh2019contextual}, and linked to self-attention itself \citep{godey2024anisotropy}. A few rogue coordinates can also dominate cosine similarity \citep{kovaleva2021bertbusters, timkey2021rogue}. One family of responses corrects the geometry after the encoder, by removing top principal components \citep{mu2018allbutthetop}, weighting tokens by frequency before pooling \citep{arora2017sif}, whitening \citep{su2021whitening, huang2021whiteningbert}, flow-based mapping \citep{li2020isotropy}, or cluster-based correction \citep{rajaee2021cluster}. Another family changes training, most often with contrastive objectives \citep{gao2021simcse}. Evaluation is concentrated on English semantic textual similarity. Multilingual analyses are rarer \citep{rajaee2022mbert}, and low-resource historical retrieval is largely unstudied. We reuse ABTT unchanged and measure what it does there.

\paragraph{Layerwise analysis of transformer representations.}
Probing work shows that surface, syntactic, and semantic information is spread across depth rather than concentrated at the output \citep{tenney2019pipeline, jawahar2019bert, liu2019linguistic}, and that the pre-training objective shapes how representations evolve \citep{voita2019bottomup}. Intermediate layers can also outperform the final layer for embedding use \citep{skean2025layer}. These profiles come from classification probes or broad English embedding suites. We report one for retrieval and align it with geometry diagnostics at the same layers.

\paragraph{Latin and classical-language NLP.}
Pre-trained models for Latin span encoder and encoder-decoder architectures \citep{bamman2020latinbert, riemenschneider2023lata}, and massively multilingual encoders also cover the language \citep{xue2021mt5, feng2022labse}, often in the Siamese bi-encoder form used here \citep{reimers2019sbert, reimers2020multilingual}. Shared tasks and linked resources organise evaluation for Latin \citep{sprugnoli2020evalatin, passarotti2024lila}, and recent work compares static and contextual embeddings on medieval Latin charters \citep{liu2024medieval}. Retrieval evaluation has centred on intertextual allusion \citep{riemenschneider2023sphilberta}. General embedding benchmarks span many tasks and languages \citep{thakur2021beir, muennighoff2023mteb} but include no medieval Latin retrieval task. We ask a different question from allusion detection: whether two fragments transmit the same legal text.

\paragraph{Text reuse and retrieval in digital humanities.}
Text reuse detection has a long history in the humanities. Systems for classical intertextuality match shared word forms and n-grams \citep{coffee2012tesserae}, large-scale alignment recovers reprinting in newspaper archives \citep{smith2013infectious}, and later work adds distributional semantics for allusive reuse, where little surface material is shared \citep{manjavacas2019allusive, burns2021intertextuality}. Evaluation is typically a ranked candidate list over a closed collection. Our routing task scores an open-set decision instead, since a fragment may belong to no existing family. That is the decision a cataloguer faces.

\paragraph{Faithfulness of token attributions.}
Integrated gradients \citep{sundararajan2017axiomatic} and learned input masks \citep{brinnerzarriess2023marc} give token-level attributions, and a large literature asks whether such attributions reflect the computation they describe \citep{jain2019attention, jacovi2020faithfully}. Standard protocols score rationales by erasure \citep{deyoung2020eraser} or by agreement with perturbation \citep{atanasova2020diagnostic}, almost always for classification. Explanation work in retrieval has concentrated on ranking models and term-level evidence \citep{anand2022xir}. We apply erasure and leave-one-out protocols to a bi-encoder cosine score, and we report where the corrected score becomes more faithful and where it does not.
