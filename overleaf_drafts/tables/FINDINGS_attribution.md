## Sanity-check target

The paper's intended claim was that **ABTT improves not only retrieval but also
explanation quality, and this holds for both MaRC (RetrievalMarK) — the
retrieval-adapted method we introduce — and IG — a standard comparator.** The
concrete threshold for the main-text story was:

> ABTT improves on ≥3 of 4 metrics for both MaRC and IG on at least one of
> {LaTa, PhilTa}.

## Recommendation

Do **not** write a main-text claim of the form "ABTT improves attribution
quality." Options for the paper, in order of decreasing confidence:

1. **Narrowed claim (safest):** ABTT improves ρ\_LOO for every model–method
   pair we evaluated (8/8), while the other three faithfulness metrics show
   mixed, encoder- and method-dependent effects. The trimmed main table
   (`attribution_metrics_main.tex`) supports this read; the appendix table
   shows the mixed picture in full.
2. **Descriptive framing:** Report the numbers without a directional ABTT
   claim and let the reader draw conclusions. The current main-table caption
   already does this.
3. **Drop the attribution-improvement angle from the contribution list and
   keep the attribution-metrics table as a diagnostic exhibit** of where
   existing methods (IG, attention-style, RetrievalMarK) succeed vs fail on
   ABTT-processed encoders, not as evidence that ABTT helps explanation.

If the revision still wants to tie attribution to retrieval gains, a
stronger path is to compute these metrics on more than 20 pairs per model
(e.g. a random test-set sample, not the curated visualization set) so that
the per-cell counts above become statistically meaningful rather than
descriptive summaries of 20-pair means.
