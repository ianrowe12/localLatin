\noindent\textbf{Reading guide.}
\textbf{Suff@25\%} ($\uparrow$): similarity retained with only the top 25\% attributed tokens.
\textbf{Comp@25\%} ($\uparrow$): similarity drop when those tokens are removed.
\textbf{Cmpct@0.8} ($\downarrow$): token fraction to recover 80\% of full-pair cosine.
$\boldsymbol{\rho_{\text{LOO}}}$ ($\uparrow$): rank correlation between attribution magnitude and true leave-one-out $\Delta\cos$.

\vspace{0.4em}

\noindent\textbf{Bottom line at 200 pairs/model.}
\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0em
\item The original ``$\geq 3/4$ metrics for both MaRC and IG on at least one T5 model'' threshold is still not met --- no cell clears 3/4.
\item $\rho_{\text{LOO}}$ now improves on \textbf{3/4} T5 cells (LaTa$\times$IG, LaTa$\times$MaRC, PhilTa$\times$IG); PhilTa$\times$MaRC regresses from $-0.036$ to $-0.120$ (a $\sim 4\sigma$ drop). So the ``ABTT universally lifts $\rho_{\text{LOO}}$'' claim from the 20-pair snapshot no longer holds.
\item The surviving robust signal: \textbf{for IG specifically}, ABTT lifts $\rho_{\text{LOO}}$ on both T5 models, and the per-cell wins are $\geq 1/4$ on every cell we looked at. That is a much narrower framing than ``ABTT improves explanation.''
\item Caveat unchanged: cross-variant deltas conflate two effects (ABTT changes both the attribution scores and the cosine being explained), so these are descriptive shifts rather than fidelity-loss measures.
\end{itemize}
